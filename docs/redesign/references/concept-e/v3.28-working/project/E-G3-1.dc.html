Why C, and what it costs

  • What makes C look confident: one bigger thing and quiet rows around it (scale contrast), not bigger rows.
  • Your revision is a gentler hero: 45 against the 32–34 dots and 19 against 17 names. It reads as the current task without taking over the screen.
  • C keeps your “Large feel” (ED-025): every row, dot and name stays as you approved it.
  • Cost of C: none in rows. Six tasks still fit above the tab bar on iPhone and Android, as in A, with 28 pt (34 dp) to spare.
  • No behaviour changes in any option. Done, open, drag and Move work the same; the hero is the same row, drawn larger.

Accessibility check

  • Targets: every option keeps 44 × 44 pt (48 dp) Done targets and rows of at least 44 pt.
  • Contrast: unchanged; colours are G3-8 and G3-9.
  • Larger text (200 %): drawn for C. Rows use minimum heights, so they grow; the hero title wraps; nothing is truncated or overlapped.
  • Screen readers: same labels and order; the hero is one list item, as today.
  • Reduced motion: nothing animated is added.

Platforms

  • iPhone: iOS chrome, tab bar and FAB unchanged; content type stays Manrope.
  • Android: M3 chrome unchanged; 48 dp targets.
  • Mac and web (Full): the Workspace grid is sized by time, so C changes nothing there; the selected now block already carries a 38 px ring. B would make block dots and names one step smaller.
  • Minimal (all platforms): unchanged; the large now-dot is the hero already.

Tablet implications (recorded, not drawn)

  • iPad and Android tablets: the same row and hero sizes; a width class never makes rows smaller.
  • Two panes: when Today sits beside another pane, the hero stays in the Today list, not in the other pane.
  • Foldables: follow the window class, not the posture.
  • Round 2: part of the tablet checklist; no tablet frames here.
