What B means on iPhone

  • B · native: the tab bar (and possibly the Day chip and menus) is a real UIKit view placed over the Flutter content, so iOS draws Liquid Glass itself.
  • Looks: the intended look is A's, but the pixels come from iOS and change with each iOS version; a board cannot draw them faithfully.
  • Cost: native Swift in the Runner, a platform view over a scrolling Flutter list, hit-testing and focus across two UI systems, and screenshots tests that cannot see the native view.
  • Unknown until a spike: scrolling smoothness under a platform view, and whether the native bar blurs Flutter content correctly.

Recommendation: A, with a native spike

  • Keeps what you approved: the glass on iPhone and the translucent bars on Android stay as drawn, with no new dependency.
  • Stays honest: labelled “approximated” (K8); the solid fallback is used when Reduce Transparency is on, on Android 11 and older, and on web.
  • Limits the risk of B: before implementation, a short spike on the iPhone tab bar only; if a native bar proves smooth and testable, that one element can move to B with an ADR.
  • Against A: it will never match iOS's own glass exactly. Against C: it drops the approved glass. Against B everywhere: highest cost and risk, and screenshot tests lose the chrome.

Accessibility and tablets

  • Reduce Transparency (iOS, Mac) and Increase Contrast: use the solid look; the fallback colours here are proposed values.
  • Contrast on glass is still unmeasured (rules §12.1); it is measured over the darkest task colour before implementation.
  • Reduce Motion: not affected.
  • iPad and Android tablets: same rule per platform; the iPad tab bar or sidebar follows Round 2.
