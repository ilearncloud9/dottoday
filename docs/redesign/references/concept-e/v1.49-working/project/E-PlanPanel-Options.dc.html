Compare · native fit and trade-offs
A · Collapsible panel: native on Mac (a collapsible split-view pane with a toolbar button and a View menu item, as Mail, Notes and Xcode do) and a standard web pattern (VS Code, Figma, Gmail). Keyboard and screen readers: one labelled button with pressed state; Hide/Show announced. Reduced motion: no slide, it appears in place.
B · Sections: works inside A or alone; native disclosure headers (Finder sidebar sections). Tabs (B3–B4) are the fallback form when space is short.
C · Sidebar only: most room, simplest window; loses "see the Inbox while dragging several tasks onto the week". Already partly true today: the sidebar has Inbox, Routines and Brain dump.
D · Auto-hide on inactivity: gives the most room without a click, and the rail keeps it discoverable. Against it: Mac and web panels do not hide on a timer (the Dock is the only common precedent), the panel can vanish mid-thought or appear when you only pass the pointer by, content moves while you work, the timing is hard for keyboard, switch and screen-reader users, and it conflicts with reduced motion. A4 already hides it automatically when space is short. If you prefer D, I would make the timer an opt-in setting on top of A.
Recommendation: A + B (bottom row). Unchanged in every option: ⌘N Capture, ⇧⌘N Brain dump, sidebar items, Day menu, ⌘K palette, the inspector and its ⌥⌘I button. If approved, it is drawn on E-FullA-v2, E-G4-3-Narrow and their dark copies, plus web (same, without the View menu).
All frames are light mode, Mac at 1280 × 800 (E-FullA-v2) and 840 × 640 (E-G4-3-Narrow); Friday's blocks in A2/A3/C1 repeat Thursday's routine to fill the extra day. Nothing on the active boards changed.
